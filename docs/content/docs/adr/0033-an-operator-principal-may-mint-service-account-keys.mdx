---
title: ADR-0033 — An operator principal may mint service-account keys
description: A principal declared in config, authenticated without a person's session, may mint and revoke keys for service accounts and nothing else.
---

Status: proposed (2026-10-29) · Supersedes: — · Superseded by: —

## Context

Minting a key needs a person's session (`notify-app/src/keys.rs`: "a key
minting keys would outlive its own revocation"), and API keys and federated
tokens are never platform admin (`auth.rs`). That is right for people, but it
leaves a Kubernetes operator unable to hand a managed service account its key:
the only way today is someone logging in and pasting the secret into a
Secret.

The operator's v0.2 `NotifyApiKey` resource needs that path, and nothing else
from the API.

## Decision

**A principal declared in config (`[[operator]]`, with its credential read from
a file) may mint, list and revoke keys for managed service accounts. It cannot
mint keys for itself or for people, create channels or accounts, or act as
platform admin.** Every mint and revocation it makes is audited under its own
id.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- A service account declared by a resource gets its key into a Kubernetes
  Secret with no human step, and loses it when the resource is deleted.

**Negative**

- A credential that mints credentials now exists. Its reach is bounded to
  managed service accounts, and revoking it means editing config, not the API.

**Accepted debt**

- The exact credential form (a static secret file versus a projected
  service-account token checked against the cluster) is left to the
  implementation; revisit once the operator's v0.1 is running.
