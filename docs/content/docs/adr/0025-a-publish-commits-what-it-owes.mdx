---
title: ADR-0025 — A publish commits what it owes, and a worker pushes
description: Sink deliveries, the idempotent replay and a fan-out outbox row are written in the publish's own transaction; the request returns after the commit and a leased worker on any replica pushes to devices and derives thumbnails.
---

Status: **accepted** (2026-10-10) · Supersedes: — · Superseded by: —

## Context

A publish committed its revision, then — still inside the HTTP request — pushed to
each device in turn (10 s timeout each), queued the channel's sinks, derived the
attachment thumbnail, and only then stored the `Idempotency-Key` response.

A request is a future, and a future gets dropped: a client that stops waiting, or
the 15 s `shutdown_timeout` running out during a `SIGTERM`. Everything after the
commit was then lost — sink deliveries, the thumbnail, and the replay. The last one
is the worst: the key stayed claimed with no response, a retry was answered `409`,
and once the claim lapsed the retry published a second notification. Serial pushes
made the window long: a channel with fifty phones behind a slow relay held the
request for minutes.

## Decision

The write carries an `AfterWrite` (`notify_ports::outbox`) and the store commits it
**in the revision's own transaction**:

- one `sink_queue` row per sink the channel routes to;
- the idempotent response, encoded against the cursor the write just took;
- one `fanout_outbox` row (keyed by that cursor) naming the notification and the
  attachment to thumbnail.

The request then pokes the bus — real-time delivery is unchanged, it was always the
event log plus `NOTIFY` — wakes the local worker, and returns.

`DrainFanout` claims outbox rows with `UPDATE … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and a
five-minute lease, pushes to a notification's devices with bounded concurrency (16),
derives the thumbnail, and deletes the row. It runs on **every** replica: a publish
wakes its own replica's worker at once, and a 5 s tick picks up rows another replica
committed and did not reach, or whose worker died.

### Amended (2026-10-10, security review)

- **Re-read at drain time.** The row names the notification; the worker reads its
  *current* state. Deleted, expired or purged: nothing is pushed. Otherwise the
  **head** revision is pushed, not the one the write committed — so two revisions
  drained out of order cannot leave the older one on a lock screen. Rows of one
  notification claimed together are collapsed into one push. Deleting or expiring a
  notification also deletes its pending rows, in the same transaction.
- **Attempts and dead rows.** Each claim counts an attempt (`attempts`), so a row
  whose worker dies without reporting still uses one up. A worker still pushing a
  row extends its lease every 60 s (back to five minutes), so a live worker is never
  raced by a second one however big the channel; a 15-minute ceiling per row
  remains as a safety net for a wedged worker. Hitting it, or a failed re-read,
  gives the row back with `last_error` and a backoff of 30 s per attempt. After 10 attempts, when it cannot be decoded (each
  row is decoded on its own — one bad row never fails a claim), or once it has
  waited an hour, the row is retired (`dead_at`) and never claimed again.
  `fanout_outbox_rows{kind}` and `fanout_outbox_oldest_age_seconds` expose the
  backlog; the leader logs when it falls behind.
- **Bounded delivery.** Each push is capped at 12 s in total, retries included, and a
  timed-out request is not retried (only refused connections, `429` and `5xx`). Rows
  are drained with bounded concurrency rather than batch by batch, so one stalled
  notification holds one slot, not the outbox.
- **Per-device progress.** Each successful push is recorded in `fanout_deliveries`
  as `(notification, revision, device)` right after the send; a retried row skips
  the devices already recorded for the head it is pushing (a newer head goes to
  everyone). The rows go when the notification's last outbox row completes, or
  with the notification (cascade) for a dead row's leftovers.
- **Idempotent digest count.** A held-back push is counted once per
  `(user, notification, revision)` (`dnd_deferrals`), however many devices or
  retries ask.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- A request dropped after the commit loses nothing; a retry with the same key gets
  the original response, never a second notification.
- A publish's latency is the transaction plus a poke, whatever the channel's device
  count or relay health.
- A crash between commit and push is recovered by whichever replica claims the row
  once its lease lapses.

**Negative**

- At least once, not exactly once — but only for the devices in flight: a crash
  between a send and its record, or a lost record, pushes those devices again on
  the retry, never the ones already recorded. Clients replace a notification by id,
  and thumbnails are overwritten with the same bytes, so the duplicate is
  invisible; the do-not-disturb tally is keyed and counts it once.
- A push shows the notification as it is when the worker reaches it, which may be a
  revision later than the write that queued it — the intent, but a re-read per
  notification on the drain path.
- The trace no longer runs `publish → fanout → push` in one tree: the fan-out span
  starts in the worker.
- Two more tables, a few more statements inside the publish transaction (which
  holds the event-log lock), and one insert per successful push on the drain path.

**Accepted debt**

- ~~The row deadline is fixed, not scaled with the device count~~ — closed
  (2026-10-10): the lease is extended while the worker pushes, and a retried row
  pushes only the devices it has not reached yet.
- Dead rows are kept for an operator for 30 days, then deleted by the purge (runbooks).
- A publish dropped *before* its commit still leaves the key claimed until the claim
  lapses (5 min), answered `409` meanwhile — never a duplicate.
